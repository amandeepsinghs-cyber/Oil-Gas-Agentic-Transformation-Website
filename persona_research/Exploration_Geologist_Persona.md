# Exploration Geologist — Persona Research

**Purpose:** which agents to build for this persona, why, what was broken before,
how it's fixed, what it's worth.

**Status:** desk research. **Not practitioner-reviewed.** See §6.

---

## 1 · The role

**Persona:** Exploration Geologist · **Segment:** Upstream

### Terminology warning — read before using this externally

**"Exploration Geologist" is predominantly a *mining* title.** Searching it
returns mineral deposits, drill programmes and orebody evaluation. In oil and gas
the same person is usually a **Petroleum Geologist**, **Exploration
Geoscientist**, **Explorationist**, or **Prospect Generator**.

Use the petroleum term in front of a petroleum audience. Getting this wrong
signals you researched the wrong industry.

### One line
> Turns a basin into a ranked set of drillable prospects, each with a defensible
> probability of success and a volume estimate — so capital can be allocated
> against geology nobody can see.

### Why their output matters
Their Pg and volume estimate is the input to the drill-or-drop decision. A
deepwater exploration well is ₹200–500 crore. The estimate is a judgment about
rock that has never been sampled, defended to a committee that cannot check it
independently.

### They do NOT own

| Not theirs | Whose |
|---|---|
| Seismic acquisition and processing | Geophysicist / contractor |
| Depth conversion, velocity model | Geophysicist |
| Log interpretation, porosity, saturation | Petrophysicist |
| Formation tops while drilling | Wellsite geologist |
| Well design and drilling programme | Drilling engineer |
| Reserves booking | Reservoir engineer, using their inputs |
| Final capital commitment | Investment committee / board |

**Note.** They *own the technical recommendation*. The approval chain above them —
exploration manager, technical assurance, investment committee, board, partners —
reviews their case but does not generate an alternative. On a wildcat that chain
is longer, because there is no production history to argue from and the case
rests almost entirely on the geological argument.

### Variants

| | **Exploration / new ventures** | **Development geologist** |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of work | Basin → play → prospect | Field, infill wells |
| Data | Sparse. Seismic, regional analogues, few wells | Dense. Many wells, production history |
| Argument rests on | Analogy and inference | Measurement |
| Timeframe | Years to a decision | Months |

**We address the exploration variant.** It is where evidence assembly matters
most, because there is no production data to fall back on.

Seniority also splits the work. Entry level is well log correlation and database
work; mid-level owns prospect generation, quantitative risking and resource
estimates for review committee; senior leads basin-scale campaigns.

#### Analogue Screening Agent · A14 · scope: estate

**The problem today**
Before risking a prospect, the geologist needs to know what this play has
actually done — which wells tested it, what they found versus what was predicted,
which chance factor failed where. The evidence is forty years of end-of-well
reports, post-well analyses, partner notes and technical memos, across systems
and formats.

**What they settle for when time runs out**
The analogues they personally remember — which skews recent, skews toward wells
they worked on, and skews toward successes, because successes get presented and
dry holes get filed. *(Inferred from the risking literature's treatment of bias,
and named as a taught topic: "common pitfalls and biases in prospect maturation."
**Needs a practitioner to confirm the mechanism.**)*

**What goes wrong, and whether anyone notices**
**Silent.** Pg is set from an incomplete sample. Nobody can tell, because
verifying it means reading everything — which is the problem. It surfaces as a
dry hole years later, attributed to geology rather than to the sample.

**Reversible?** No. Once the well is drilled the money is spent.

**What the agent does**
Given a prospect — play, trap type, depth, area — identifies the relevant
historical well population across the document estate, extracts predicted versus
actual per chance factor, and returns the honest base rate for that play with
each well cited to report and page.

**What the human keeps**
Which analogues genuinely apply, and the Pg. The agent does not risk anything.

**What it's worth**
1–2 weeks per prospect `?`, and more importantly coverage — from what one person
could read to the full population. If the true base rate in a play is 1 in 5 and
the team carries 1 in 3, that gap is the difference between a good bet and a bad
one across a campaign.

**Evidence** — workflow literature confirms analogue use in risking; the time
figure is `inferred`; the recall-bias mechanism `needs a practitioner`.

**Best demo or business case?** Business case. Hard to demo without a real
document corpus.

#### Contradiction Check Agent · A17 · scope: estate

**The problem today**
The seismic interpretation, the petrophysical summary, the geochemistry and the
offset well reports each say something about the prospect. They are produced by
different people, in different formats, at different times. **They converge only
inside one person's head** — which is what the geologist's own integration slide
already says.

**What they settle for when time runs out**
Reading the summary sections and trusting the specialists. Contradictions buried
in the body of a report survive to the review.

**What goes wrong** **Silent.** The map says structural high, the offset report
says the sand pinches out, and nobody put the two sentences next to each other.

**What the agent does**
Reads all four sources and flags where they disagree, sourced to report and page.
It does not adjudicate.

**What the human keeps** Whether a flagged contradiction is real, and what it
means for the prospect.

**What it's worth** This is not time saved — **it is work that does not happen
today at all**, because doing it means one person reading everything. That is a
different and stronger argument than efficiency.

**Evidence** — `inferred` from the multi-dataset integration problem, which is
well documented. The *frequency* of real contradictions `needs a practitioner`.

**Best demo or business case?** **Best demo.** An agent disagreeing with a stated
assumption, with receipts, is a thing nobody in the room has seen.

#### Post-Well Look-Back Agent · A21, A22 · scope: estate

**The problem today**
Post-well audits to refine future risk assessment are standard taught practice.
In reality they are the first thing dropped, because the team has already moved
to the next well.

**What they settle for** It doesn't get done, or it produces a filed report
nobody reads before the next risking exercise.

**What goes wrong** **Silent, and repeating.** If well 1 failed on seal and three
queued prospects rest on the same seal assumption, the campaign drills the same
mistake twice.

**Reversible?** The learning is recoverable; the second dry hole is not.

**What the agent does**
On well completion: compares prognosis to outcome per chance factor, identifies
which failed, and **flags every other prospect in the portfolio resting on the
same assumption.**

**What the human keeps** Why it failed, and which prospects to re-risk.

**What it's worth** One avoided repeat dry hole. At ₹200–500 crore, a single
catch pays for the whole programme. Also the only agent here that closes a
learning loop rather than speeding one up.

**Evidence** — post-well audit is documented practice; *that it is routinely
skipped* is `inferred` and **needs a practitioner**. This matters: if they do it
diligently, this agent is worth much less.

#### Risking Calibration Agent · A15 + A21 · scope: portfolio
**The strongest find from this research, and it was not on my earlier list.**

**The problem today**
Probabilistic risking became industry norm in the 1990s, partly because
deterministic exploration was destroying value. But **nobody systematically
checks whether their own Pg numbers have been right.** Training courses name
"common pitfalls and biases in prospect maturation" as a topic, which tells you
the bias is recognised — and recognising a bias is not the same as measuring it.

**What they settle for** Individual judgment, prospect by prospect, with no
feedback loop against outcomes.

**What goes wrong** **Silent and systematic.** If a team's Pg runs optimistic by
ten points across forty wells, every capital decision in that period was
mispriced — and the error is invisible at the level of any single well.

**What the agent does**
Across the well history: compares assigned Pg against actual outcomes, by chance
factor, by play, by assessor. Returns the calibration curve. *Assigned 0.35 on
this play across nineteen wells; observed success 0.21. The gap is concentrated
in seal.*

**What the human keeps** Everything. It reports; it risks nothing.

**What it's worth** This is the one an exploration head will care about most,
because it is the only thing here that speaks directly to whether the portfolio's
numbers can be trusted. No monolith does it — Petrel risks a prospect, it does
not audit the risker.

**Evidence** — the bias problem is documented. *That operators do not already
measure calibration* is my `inference` and is the **single most important thing
to check.** Some majors do. If this operator does, the agent is redundant.

**Best demo or business case?** Business case, strongly. Needs historical
outcome data to demo.

#### Prospect Pack Assembly Agent · A19, A20 · scope: prospect

**The problem today** Prospect summary sheets for management approval are a named
deliverable. Assembled manually, each time, from work scattered across the
maturation process.

**What the agent does** Drafts the pack: evidence table, chance factors with their
basis, analogue summary, volumes, sourced to page — and **flags where the stated
risking contradicts the historical record** before the meeting rather than during it.

**What the human keeps** The conclusion, the recommendation, the defence.

**What it's worth** Days per prospect, plus fewer unanswerable challenges in the
room. **Same machinery as the petrophysicist's report agent** — build once.

### Not building, and why

| Action | Why not |
|---|---|
| A5, A6 — play fairway and CRS mapping | The patent literature describes re-computing these maps as painstaking, so the pain is real — but map generation is squarely monolith territory (Petrel, Trinity, and the tools that patent describes). **We assemble the evidence behind the risk factors; we do not draw the maps.** |
| A2 — petroleum systems modelling | Specialist basin modelling software. Not ours. |
| A9, A10, A16 — lead maturation, trap definition, volumetrics | Petrel. Interpretation and computation. |
| A15 — assigning Pg | Where professional credibility sits. Calibrating it afterwards is fine; assigning it is not. |
| A4, A7 — play definition and play COS | Judgment on sparse evidence. Feed it, don't make it. |

**The line to say:** *We don't risk your prospects. We make sure the number you
assign is priced against every well this play has already tested.*

## 7 · Research notes

### Lines worth remembering

> **From a patent filing on prospect assessment tools:** if a geological condition
> changes, or the explorationist changes an assumption underpinning a chance map,
> the map must be reconfigured and recalculated — a painstaking process, and the
> sheer volume of input data makes creation difficult and non-intuitive.
> *A documented admission of the pain, from people selling the software.*

> Probabilistic risking became industry norm in the 1990s largely because of
> **perceived value destruction from drilling on deterministic judgment.** The
> industry has already accepted, once, that better-founded risking is worth
> changing practice for.

> On a wildcat there is no production history. **The analogues are not supporting
> evidence — they are the argument.**

> Post-well audits and bias in prospect maturation are both *taught topics*.
> The profession knows the problem. It has not solved it.

### Contradictions between sources
- "Exploration Geologist" returns mining; petroleum sources use Petroleum
  Geologist / Explorationist. Resolved in §1.
- Job specs put most time on seismic interpretation (B2, Petrel); workflow
  literature emphasises integration and risking (B1). Likely a seniority split —
  junior interprets, senior integrates and risks. **Unresolved; affects who the
  agents are for.**

### Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Play | A region where accumulations of a given type may occur; a conceptual model of a style of accumulation |
| Prospect | A single drillable location |
| Lead | An immature prospect, not yet defined enough to drill |
| Pg / COS | Probability of geological success / chance of success |
| Chance factors | Reservoir, charge, seal, trap, timing — all must work |
| Play fairway | The mapped area where a play's elements are expected to be present |
| CRS mapping | Common Risk Segment — stacking element risk maps into a composite |
| YTF | Yet-to-find — undiscovered resource estimated for a play or basin |
| Prospect maturation | Progressing a lead to a drillable, risked prospect |
| Risk vs uncertainty | Risk = will it work at all (Pg). Uncertainty = how big if it does |
